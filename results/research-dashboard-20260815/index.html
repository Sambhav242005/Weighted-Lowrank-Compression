How to Read This Dashboard

This dashboard presents experimental evidence about compressing neural network models. It answers one big question: Can we shrink AI models without making them significantly dumber?

  • Each section answers a specific question — scroll through to see what was tested and what happened.
  • Green = positive result, Orange = mixed, Red = negative result — the colored badges next to each experiment tell you the verdict at a glance.
  • PPL (Perplexity) is the main score. It measures how confused the model is when predicting text. Lower is better. A PPL of 36 means the model is roughly 36× unsure about each word — while a PPL of 15,000 means it's basically guessing randomly.
  • Don't worry about the math — each section includes a plain-English takeaway so you don't need to read the numbers to understand the结论.

Key Concepts (What Do These Terms Mean?)

PPL (Perplexity)
A score that measures how well a language model predicts text. Lower is better. A PPL of 36 is good; a PPL of 15,000 means the model is basically broken.
Think of it like a quiz score — but backwards. Lower = fewer mistakes.
SVD (Singular Value Decomposition)
A mathematical tool that breaks a complex matrix into simpler parts. It's the standard way to compress a matrix by keeping only the most important patterns.
Like compressing a photo by keeping only the main colors and discarding the rest.
Low-Rank Approximation
Replacing a large matrix with a smaller, simpler one that captures the most important structure. "Rank" controls how many patterns are kept — higher rank = more detail but less compression.
Like summarizing a 300-page book into 30 pages — you keep the key ideas but lose some details.
Activation-Weighted Fitting
Our method: instead of compressing all weights equally, focus compression on the weights the model actually uses a lot (the "high-traffic" ones). This preserves quality where it matters most.
Like prioritizing highway repairs over side street repairs — keep the busy roads smooth, even if some quiet streets get bumpy.
Rank-k
How many "directions" or patterns the compressed version keeps. Rank-384 means keeping 384 main patterns out of potentially thousands. Higher k = more faithful to original but bigger file.
Like choosing 384 colors to paint a scene instead of the full palette of 10,000 — you get 95% of the beauty at 4% of the cost.
Pruning
Deleting weights from the model to make it smaller. "Magnitude pruning" deletes the smallest weights. "Activation-aware pruning" deletes weights the model rarely uses.
Like cleaning out a closet — magnitude pruning throws away the smallest items; activation-aware throws away things you never wear.
Current decision.

On the pinned Gemma-3-1B run, our activation-weighted method produced a PPL of about 62 (only 6.7% worse than the original 58). Meanwhile, plain SVD — the standard approach — produced a PPL of 251 (333% worse than baseline, and basically unusable). This supports an empirical advantage over SVD, but not algorithmic novelty until ASVD/EoRA controls are complete.

Translation: Our method keeps the model almost as smart as the original. The standard method (SVD) makes it 4× dumber. We're still checking if this is a new invention or just a known technique applied differently.

Representation decision

Keep the original activation-weighted low-rank direction. The pruning and reference-neuron experiments are negative controls, not replacements.

In plain English: We tested two alternatives (pruning and reference neurons). Both failed. So we're sticking with our main approach.

Next experiment

Run matched ASVD/EoRA on the same Gemma protocol, then test weighted low-rank plus packed int4 residuals using the actual inference representation.

In plain English: We need to compare our method against the two most similar published techniques (ASVD and EoRA) to see if we're actually doing something new, or just reinventing existing work.

Reproducibility

Model and dataset revisions are pinned. Each run writes to a unique results directory; prior artifacts are preserved.

In plain English: We saved every experiment's inputs and outputs so anyone can re-run our tests and verify the results. Nothing is overwritten.
