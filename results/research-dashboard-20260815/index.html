Abstract

Large language models use large matrices, or tables of numbers, to turn text into predictions. Replacing some of these matrices with two smaller ones can save storage, but the replacements may change how information passes through the model. We study whether a compression method guided by the model's observed inputs can preserve its predictions and the internal signals passed between layers. We compare this method with a version that also penalizes changes to the original weights, using the same number of stored values. Tests on three pretrained language models show that the penalty provides no consistent improvement in both prediction quality and internal similarity. In one model, prediction quality improves while average error in these internal signals grows to 3.86 times that of the method without the penalty. Tests on consecutive layers also find a group where both prediction quality and internal similarity worsen for all three choices of fitting data. Separate storage and timing tests remove 29.17% of the weights in the selected matrices, but reduce the complete model file by only 0.894%; execution time varies with the workload. These findings show that prediction quality alone cannot establish whether compression preserves the original computation. Failed numerical accuracy checks, incomplete additional tests for one model, and evaluation on a previously used text dataset make the findings exploratory. A practical compression claim therefore needs separate evidence for prediction quality, internal behavior, storage, and speed.

1. What the study asks

Can a pretrained transformer use fewer stored weights while retaining the original model’s behavior? We replace selected attention-output matrices with two smaller factors and measure both next-token prediction and the internal signals passed between blocks.

Current finding. Activation-aware fitting improves on plain SVD in the tested Gemma setting. Adding the weight-space anchor gives no consistent improvement in both prediction quality and residual fidelity across Gemma, Qwen and LFM. A better likelihood score alone cannot establish preservation of the original computation.

2. What is compared

A dense projection computes Wx + b. Its compact replacement computes U(Vx) + b, applying the original bias once. At a fixed factor budget, covariance whitening weights input directions using calibration activations. The anchored version adds a penalty for changing the original weights.

Jβ(A) = ‖(W − A)X‖²F + βn‖W − A‖²F, with A = UV

The matched whitening component uses β = ρ = 0; the selected anchor uses β = 0.03 and ρ = 0. The charts label that measured anchored fit Ours (anchored) in orange. Gemma’s primary rank is 384, Qwen’s is 483, and LFM’s is 725. Other layers and hybrid mixers remain dense.

Protocol and interpretation

The historical quality cohorts use calibration seeds 11, 23 and 37, paired across methods. WikiText-2 supplies calibration, validation and test splits; the test set has already influenced the project, so these are regression results. The later BF16 practical cohort uses only seeds 11 and 23 and supplies no new drift measurements.

Exact-arithmetic formulas require their stated rank and spectral assumptions. Failed finite-precision controls prevent attributing all measured changes solely to the mathematical objective. The current paper documents measured-output fitting, extra ridge and spectral cutoffs separately.

4. Results

The charts use the same saved numbers as the current IEEE source. On a narrow screen, scroll a chart horizontally or open its exact-value table. NLL is negative log-likelihood; lower NLL means better prediction on the scored text. Residual drift measures internal-state difference from the original model.

The anchor’s mean Gemma NLL is 3.565921 versus 3.572814 for whitening. Its average drift is slightly lower, but drift worsens in two of three paired seeds. The average does not establish a reproducible joint improvement.

LFM shows why the metrics must be assessed separately: the anchor improves likelihood in every seed while mean residual drift rises to about 3.86 times the whitening mean. Lower NLL does not establish preservation, denoising, or a particular causal mechanism.

6. Limitations and evidence boundaries

  • The strict numerical-readiness checks failed. The results describe recorded implementations; they cannot isolate ideal mathematical objectives from numerical error.
  • Qwen has all eight primary-test records but only 10 of 18 validation-frontier records. Missing measurements remain missing.
  • WikiText-2 has already influenced the project. No fresh-domain confirmation, downstream task accuracy, or free-running generation equivalence has been established.
  • Three calibration seeds do not represent independently pretrained models. The later BF16 practical cohort has only two seeds and no new drift measurements.
  • Only the selected attention-output matrices are studied. Results do not establish preservation for MLPs, hybrid mixers, larger models, or other rank allocations.
  • Model file size, CUDA allocator peaks and warmed-up latency are distinct measurements. Storage savings alone do not establish practical speed or memory savings.

7. Future Scope

A small neural network for compression

A proposed next direction is a small bottleneck neural network that learns a compact mapping for selected dense projections from the original model’s activations. A second option is a small correction network alongside lower-rank factors.

Every retained factor, network weight and bias must count toward a matched storage budget. Any local improvement must be checked for held-out prediction quality and residual drift after replacements are composed across layers, alongside complete file size, peak memory and warmed-up latency. Neither network has been implemented or tested here.

Numerical controls and broader evaluation

Resolve failed controls and complete missing Qwen measurements before controlled comparisons of group-output matching. Fresh evaluation data, downstream tasks and generation tests need frozen splits, budgets and decision criteria. Fused factor execution and runtime-informed rank allocation remain further hypotheses.

TinyBERT is a related teacher–student distillation precedent; it does not establish the proposed projection replacement’s effectiveness. The paper’s full Future Scope is Section 8, including the small-network proposal in Section 8.1.
